> **In brief:**
> 1. Four accessories are enough to start: **heat deflector**, **protective cover**, **core temperature probe** and **cast iron grate**. They cover almost every kind of cooking.
> 2. The **deflector** is the only truly non-negotiable one: without it, the kamado only does direct cooking and loses most of its point.
> 3. The **core probe** is the most underrated: the dome thermometer measures air temperature, not meat temperature.
> 4. Full bundles sold with the kamado are rarely a good deal: half the accessories never get used.

A kamado usually comes with an accessory list as long as your arm, and the buyer has no way of knowing which ones actually matter. The answer is shorter than expected: four pieces cover almost every use, and the order in which they are bought matters as much as the list itself.

## The four essentials, in order

### The heat deflector, the only non-negotiable one

It is a ceramic plate that slides between the coals and the grate. It blocks direct radiation from the charcoal and turns the kamado into a convection oven: heat circulates around the food instead of attacking it from below.

Without a deflector, a kamado does one thing only, grill hard and fast. With one, it roasts, it cooks slowly, it bakes bread. It is the piece that unlocks half the repertoire, so it comes first, before even a second grate.

### The cover, as soon as the kamado stays outside

Ceramic handles frost, but the metal parts, the hinge and the sealing band age much faster when exposed to rain and sun. A cover costs little compared with the price of a kamado and pushes back the replacement of wear parts by several years.

### The core temperature probe, the most underrated

The dome thermometer shows the air temperature inside the chamber. That is not the temperature of the meat, and the gap is considerable on a thick cut. On a long cook, steering by the dome thermometer is like driving without a speedometer: it is the leading cause of meat overcooked or pulled too early.

A two-sensor probe, one in the chamber and one in the food, is the best value-for-money item on the whole list.

### The cast iron grate, if you grill often

Cast iron stores heat and releases it slowly, which gives clean, even marking. Stainless steel runs cooler in inertia but cleans effortlessly and does not rust. If you grill meat every week, cast iron is worth the investment and the care it requires. Otherwise the original stainless grate is more than enough.

## The ones that can wait

### The pizza stone

It only makes sense above 300 degrees Celsius, and assumes you already know how to steer the air vents to reach and hold that range. Excellent accessory, poor first purchase.

### The cold smoke generator

A stainless steel spiral filled with sawdust that produces cold smoke for several hours without lighting the charcoal. Essential for smoking salmon or cheese, useless for everything else. Buy it the day you decide to smoke, not before.

### The extension grate

It adds a second level above the main grate and clearly increases surface. On a long cook, thick cuts go low, close to the source, and thin cuts high where heat is gentler. It is a volume accessory: useful when cooking for a crowd, not when learning. Watch compatibility with the exact diameter of the kamado, grates are not interchangeable between sizes.

### The rotisserie

Spectacular, but the most specialised item on the list. Best kept for those who cook whole poultry regularly.

## What matters more than the list

### The buying order

Deflector, cover, probe, cast iron grate. Then, depending on what you actually cook rather than what you imagine cooking: pizza stone, smoke generator, extension grate, rotisserie. This order follows real use, not the catalogue.

### Compatibility with your model

A kamado accessory is not universal. Internal diameter, firebox shape and height under the dome vary from one maker to another, and sometimes from one size to another within the same maker. Check the exact reference before buying a deflector or a grate: it is the leading cause of product returns.

### Do not buy the full bundle

This is the most common and most expensive mistake. The bundle offered alongside the kamado looks like a deal, but it always contains two or three pieces that will never leave the box, because they match cooking styles you will not adopt. Four accessories that get used beat eight where half sit idle.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which accessories are essential for a kamado?

Four accessories are enough to start and cover almost every kind of cooking. The **ceramic heat deflector**, which takes the kamado from direct to indirect cooking: without it, roasting a chicken or running a slow cook is impossible. A **cast iron cooking grate**, which reaches higher temperatures and marks food better than stainless steel. A **protective cover**, as soon as the kamado stays outdoors. And a **core temperature probe**, because the dome thermometer measures air, not meat. The rest comes later depending on how you actually cook.

### What is the purpose of a heat deflector in a kamado?

The deflector is a ceramic plate that sits between the coals and the grate. It blocks direct radiation from the charcoal and turns the kamado into a convection oven: heat circulates around the food instead of grilling it from below. That is what makes it possible to cook a roast, a whole chicken, bread or a slow-cooked cut without burning the surface. Without a deflector, a kamado only does high-heat direct cooking, which uses a fraction of its capability.

### Cast iron or stainless steel grate for a kamado?

Cast iron stores heat and releases it slowly, which gives clean, even marking on meat and withstands high temperatures without warping. Stainless steel has less thermal inertia but cleans more easily and needs no particular care. In practice, cast iron is preferable as soon as you grill meat regularly, stainless steel suits more occasional and versatile use. Cast iron does need seasoning and drying after washing, otherwise it rusts.

### How do you use an extension grate on a kamado?

An extension grate adds a second cooking level above the main grate, which significantly increases available surface without changing kamado. It is mostly useful for long cooks and large quantities: thick cuts go on the lower level, close to the source, thin cuts and vegetables on the upper level where heat is gentler. Combined with the deflector, it allows indirect cooking on two tiers. Compatibility with the exact diameter of your kamado has to be checked, as grates are not interchangeable between sizes.

### In which order should you buy kamado accessories?

The order that avoids paying twice: heat deflector, protective cover, temperature probe, then cast iron grate. Those four are used from the first weeks and on almost every cook. Then, depending on what you actually cook, comes the pizza stone if you aim for very high temperature, the cold smoke generator if you want to smoke, the extension grate if you cook for a crowd, and the rotisserie last. The classic mistake is buying a full bundle at the same time as the kamado: half the accessories never leave the box.
