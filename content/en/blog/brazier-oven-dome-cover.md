## Why a simple cover is not enough

The question keeps coming up among plancha brazier owners: can you put a cover on and cook underneath it like in an oven? The honest answer is no. A classic brazier cover is a protective accessory: it shelters the fire bowl from rain and leaves when not in use, or acts as a snuffer to extinguish the embers at the end of the evening. Placed over an active fire, it has no controlled air intake, no door for loading and no thermometer: the combustion chokes and nothing cooks properly.

To cook oven-style, you need the opposite: trapping the heat without killing the fire. A raised dome that lets the fire breathe, circulates hot air around the food and lets you monitor the temperature. That is exactly the difference between a cover and a cooking dome, and it is why searching for a "cover to turn a brazier into an oven" actually leads to another family of accessories: the brazier oven.

### What makes a real oven on a brazier

Four elements separate a serious cooking dome from a simple domed sheet of metal. A door, first, to load and remove dishes without lifting the whole burning-hot assembly. A built-in thermometer, because pizza needs fierce heat and bread a gentler one. Air management, to keep combustion going under the dome. And a suitable cooking surface, pizza stone or cast iron plate, because a grate alone suits neither dough nor dishes.

## The alternatives: warming cloche and standalone oven

The stainless steel cooking cloche, placed directly on the plancha, remains the cheapest option at around 60 to 150 euros. It is handy for melting cheese, finishing a gentle cook or keeping food warm, but it quickly reaches its limits: no thermometer, no door, no stone, and heat coming only from below. For a pizza or a bread, the result is nothing like an oven.

The standalone pizza oven cooks very well, but it does not transform the brazier: it is a second piece of equipment, with its own fire to light, fuel to manage and space to find on the terrace, for a budget from 300 euros to well over 1,000 euros depending on the model. ### The reflex before buying: measure the log opening

Compatibility is the criterion everyone discovers too late. A cooking dome sits above the central opening of the brazier, the log opening: if the diameters do not match, the dome is unstable or chokes the fire. The COEO kit covers log openings from 39 to 50 cm, which corresponds to its TRIO 82 and TRIO 98 and to a good share of plancha braziers on the market. When in doubt, the manufacturer recommends checking compatibility before purchase. The brazier's material makes no difference here: the differences between finishes are detailed in our comparison of [Corten steel versus black steel for a brazier](/en/blog/corten-or-black-steel-brazier/).

## How to choose your solution in three questions

What do you want to cook? Pizzas, breads and casserole dishes call for a real dome with stone and thermometer; melting cheese only needs a cloche. Do you already own a brazier you love? If so, a compatible dome enhances what you have, whereas a standalone oven competes with it. What is your budget? At 349 euros for the complete kit, stone and plate included, converting the brazier costs less than a standalone oven of equivalent quality, and naturally completes a setup built for outdoor cooking, like those in our guide on [which brazier to choose to cook outdoors](/en/blog/which-brazier-to-cook-outdoors/).

### Mistakes to avoid

1. Buying a flat cover expecting to cook under it: it is made to protect and smother, not to cook.
2. Ignoring the log opening diameter: an incompatible dome is unstable and cooks poorly.
3. Putting the pizza directly on the plancha without a dome or stone: the base burns underneath while the topping stays raw.
